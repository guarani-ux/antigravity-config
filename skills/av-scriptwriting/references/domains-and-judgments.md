# The Six Converging Domains & Practitioner Judgments in A/V Scriptwriting

An Audio-Video (A/V) script sits at the intersection of six distinct professional disciplines. When a script fails, it is almost always because one discipline dominated and erased the others.

---

## 1. Communications Practice (Public-Service & Stakeholder Literacy)

- **Core Contract:** Enable stakeholders and the public to understand, trust, and act on what an organization provides.
- **Key Judgments:**
  1. **Institutional-to-Human Translation:** Organizations think in internal programs and frameworks (*"5 Early Literacy Practices," "Strategic Pillar 3"*). Audiences think in lived problems (*"Where can I take my toddler when it's minus twenty without spending \$30?"*). Translate internal vocabulary into human task language while preserving factual accuracy.
  2. **Message Hierarchy over Committee Stuffing:** When a brief contains five stakeholder talking points, giving all five equal weight guarantees the viewer remembers none. Select one primary emotional/practical spine and nest the remaining points inside it as supporting proof.
  3. **Early Clarity vs. Deferred Discovery:** For urgent practical instructions (e.g., safety notices, service changes), place the required action at the very beginning. For community portraits, documentaries, and brand storytelling, orient the viewer immediately to the human stakes while letting deeper meaning unfold through the story.

---

## 2. Marketing, Content Strategy & YouTube Literacy

- **Core Contract:** Earn voluntary attention from a viewer who can leave in one second, and remove friction between interest and action.
- **Key Judgments:**
  1. **Levels of Awareness (Schwartz / CCN Gate):**
     - *New (Stranger):* Has no prior loyalty and may hold misconceptions. The opening 5–10 seconds must hook them via a relatable human tension, visual surprise, or contrarian truth.
     - *Casual:* Knows the brand exists, needs a concrete reason why this specific offering matters right now.
     - *Core:* Rewards depth and honesty; alienated by shallow hype.
  2. **Packaging Alignment:** The opening visual frame and first spoken sentence must immediately validate the curiosity that made the viewer click, without wasting 15 seconds on logos, throat-clearing greetings (*"Hi, welcome back to..."*), or institutional history.
  3. **Friction-Free Closing:** In public-service and community media, the Call to Action (CTA) is not a hard sales pitch; it is **barrier removal** (*"No registration. No fees. Just walk in."*).

---

## 3. A/V Scriptwriting Literacy (Dual-Channel Composition)

- **Core Contract:** Compose across two simultaneous sensory channels (Eye and Ear) over linear time where the audience cannot re-read a sentence.
- **Key Judgments:**
  1. **Writing for the Ear (Orality):**
     - Print sentences rely on subordinate clauses, passive voice, and abstract nouns.
     - Spoken audio requires **one idea per breath**, active verbs, contractions, and concrete nouns.
     - Test every line by reading it out loud at a natural conversational pace. If the speaker runs out of breath or sounds like they are reading a brochure, rewrite it.
  2. **Production Custody & Source Honesty:**
     - Distinguish **authored narration (`[V.O.]` / `[ON-CAMERA]`)**, **unfilmed interview targets (`[SOUNDBITE TARGET]`)**, and **recorded verbatim quotes**.
     - Never fabricate verbatim documentary quotes and present them as already-recorded evidence.
     - When adapting factual or demographic research into a script, keep population counts, dates, and attributions accurate without inflating them into unsupported claims.

## 4. Filmmaking & Directing Practice

- **Core Contract:** Turn abstract themes into observable human behavior, emotional shifts, and authentic performance.
- **Key Judgments:**
  1. **Directing Non-Actors & Subject-Matter Experts (SMEs):**
     - Real specialists and community members tighten up if forced to memorize dense paragraphs on camera.
     - Keep direct-to-lens `[ON-CAMERA]` standup lines short (1–2 punchy sentences).
     - Use open-ended **Interview Elicitation Prompts** to get SMEs telling stories in their own natural cadence (*"Walk me through what you actually see happen when..."* rather than *"Can you explain our literacy mandate?"*).
  2. **Eliciting Community Voices (Bench / Street Grabs):**
     - Never ask a member of the public an abstract institutional question (*"How does the library foster community?"*).
     - Ask specific, sensory, lived questions (*"When it hits minus twenty-five in January and you have a toddler, what does walking through those doors feel like?"*).
  3. **Walter Murch's Rule of Six:**
     - Cut and structure for **Emotion (51%)** and **Story (23%)** first, **Rhythm (10%)** and **Eye-Trace (7%)** next. Never add unmotivated cuts or decorative graphics that clutter the emotional turn.

## 5. Videography & Cinematography Practice

- **Core Contract:** Make explicit optical, spatial, and coverage decisions in the `VIDEO` column so the crew shoots with emotional intent rather than collecting generic B-roll.
- **Key Judgments:**
  1. **Camera Height & Optical Axis:**
     - Standing adult height (5'8" looking down) feels detached and clinical.
     - Drop to **28–32 inches** for young children so the viewer inhabits their scale.
     - Shoot seated portraits at eye level or **15–25mm below eye-line** for warmth and dignity.
  2. **Focal Length & Psychological Distance:**
     - **`24mm–35mm` (Close Proximity):** Immersive presence inside active spaces.
     - **`50mm`:** Neutral human observational perspective.
     - **`85mm–135mm` (Candid Compression):** Isolates intimate moments between parents and children from across a room without breaking their natural behavior.
  3. **Motivated Movement vs. Stillness:**
     - Lock the tripod off when human action inside the frame is strong.
     - Move the lens only across thresholds, during slow emotional push-ins, or when following a subject's gaze/action reveal.
  4. **The 5-Shot Coverage Sequence:**
     - Cover every physical action in 5 matching angles for the editor: *(1) Tight on hands, (2) Tight on eyes/face, (3) Wide master context, (4) Over-the-shoulder POV, (5) Creative foreground/architecture angle*.
  5. **Edit Handles:** Hold static frames 7–10 seconds; hold 3–4 seconds of silent eye contact after an interviewee finishes speaking to capture the real micro-expression.

---

## 6. Audio Engineering & Sound Design Practice

- **Core Contract:** Construct a dimensional acoustic space where spoken voice, room tone, diegetic SFX, and music work together without masking one another.
- **Key Judgments:**
  1. **Diegetic Sound as Immediate Proof:** Open pieces with authentic room acoustics and specific spot sounds (`[NAT SOUND]`) before music or voiceover enters. Physical sound convinces the brain that the space is real.
  2. **J-Cuts & L-Cuts:** Never cut picture and audio on the exact same frame across every beat. Let incoming dialogue or room sound lead the visual cut by 1–2 seconds (J-cut), or let a speaker's final phrase trail over the next visual reveal (L-cut).
  3. **Frequency & Dynamic Carving:** Keep music beds sparse in the `800 Hz – 3 kHz` vocal presence band, and script explicit 2–3 second narration pauses so natural laughter, footsteps, or mechanical clicks can rise to the foreground.
