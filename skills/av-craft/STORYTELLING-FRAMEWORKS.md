## 1. What Storytelling Is (Versus Information & Description)

Weak scripts confuse three distinct modes of writing:
- **Information (Static):** Facts arranged by category. *("CPL opened its first Early Learning Centre in 2016 and now has 13 locations teaching Talk, Sing, Read, Write, and Play.")*
- **Description (Sensory):** Observations arranged in space. *("Sunlight hits the foam ramps as children run in socks.")*
- **Story (Dynamic):** **A causally linked sequence of changes in a human state—or in the audience's understanding—driven by tension between how things are and how they could be.**

If nothing **changes**—either in a person's lived predicament on screen or in how the viewer perceives the subject—a story has not occurred.

In non-fiction, institutional, and documentary video, transformation operates across two channels:
1. **On-Screen Transformation (The Subject):** A real person moves from one lived state to another (e.g., an isolated parent in winter stepping into a warm room and exhaling; a toddler struggling with a wooden puzzle until it clicks).
2. **In-Seat Transformation (The Viewer):** The viewer enters holding a surface assumption (*"That's just a noisy indoor playground"*), encounters concrete evidence that disrupts that assumption, and leaves with a transformed understanding.

## 2. The Six Foundational Storytelling Frameworks

### Framework 1: Sheila Curran Bernard — *Topic vs. Story Spine*
*(From Documentary Storytelling)*
- **The Principle:** A **Topic** is a static subject area (*"Early Learning Centres," "Library Digital Archives," "Summer Reading Club"*). A **Story Spine** is an active, forward-moving progression with human stakes, tension, and discovery.
- **Diagnostic:** When a writer organizes from a Topic, they build **thematic buckets** (*Bucket 1: Kids; Bucket 2: Parents; Bucket 3: History*) and glue them together with artificial transitions. When a writer organizes from a Story Spine, all institutional requirements become stops along a single unfolding journey.

### Framework 2: Ira Glass — *The Anecdote Train + The Moment of Reflection*
*(From This American Life / Broadcast Non-Fiction)*
- **The Principle:** Compelling broadcast narrative alternates between two tracks:
  1. **The Anecdote (The Train):** Concrete sequence of observable actions (*"This happened, which led to that, which led to the next thing"*). Putting specific actions in sequence creates forward momentum that makes the audience ask: *What happens next?*
  2. **The Moment of Reflection (The Meaning):** Once the concrete human moment has earned emotional investment, step back and name the larger truth it reveals.
- **Diagnostic:** Institutional brochure scripts fail because they are **100% Reflection/Claim and 0% Anecdote**. They state abstract conclusions (*"Play builds neural pathways"*) without first putting the viewer inside a specific, observable moment on the floor.

### Framework 3: Robert McKee & Shawn Coyne — *The Gap & Polarity Value Shifts*
*(From Story & The Story Grid)*
- **The Principle:**
  - **The Gap:** Story lives in the mismatch between **expectation** (what a character or viewer expects) and **reality** (what actually happens).
  - **Value Shifts:** Every single beat must shift an emotional or epistemic value charge from negative to positive ($-\rightarrow +$) or from surface to deep (e.g., *Chaos $\rightarrow$ Order*, *Isolation $\rightarrow$ Connection*, *Toy $\rightarrow$ Tool*). If a beat begins and ends in the same value state, it has no narrative function.

### Framework 4: Aristotle / Parker & Stone — *Causal ("But / Therefore") vs. Episodic ("And Then")*
*(From Poetics & screenwriting practice)*
- **The Principle:** If Beat 1 and Beat 2 are joined by **"AND ALSO"** or **"AND THEN,"** the script is an episodic list. In a story, Beat 1 forces Beat 2 into existence via **"BUT"** (contradiction, deeper truth, obstacle) or **"THEREFORE / BECAUSE"** (consequence, human dependency).

### Framework 5: Eisenstein, Kuleshov & Ken Burns — *Montage & "1 + 1 = 3" Juxtaposition*
*(Audiovisual Narrative Theory)*
- **The Principle:** Film creates meaning in the **collision between Shot A and Shot B** and between **Image ($1$) and Sound ($1$)**.
- **Diagnostic:** If the voiceover literally describes what is on screen, $1 + 1 = 1$ (redundant See-Say). When the camera shows a parent's shoulders dropping on a bench ($1$) while the audio carries the bright chaos of twenty toddlers playing safely ten feet away ($1$), the viewer's mind constructs the third meaning ($3$): *sanctuary*.

### Framework 6: Green & Brock / Loewenstein — *Narrative Transportation & Curiosity Gaps*
*(Cognitive Narrative Psychology)*
- **The Principle:** Audiences are not transported by statistics or broad generalizations (*"thousands of Calgarians"*); they are transported by **sensory particularity** (one child's hands, the sound of winter boots coming off, two parents making eye contact). Sustained attention requires opening a concrete question early and resolving it progressively through lived proof.

## 3. Case Study: Transforming a Brochure into a Causal Story

Below is how the storytelling engine rescues a multi-stakeholder brief (4 required pillars: *early literacy, parent community, 10-year anniversary, free drop-in access*) from feeling crammed:

| Dimension | The Brochure Approach (Crammed Buckets) | The Causal Story Spine Approach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Structure** | **Bucket 1:** Kids play & learn literacy.<br>*Transition: "And it's not just for kids..."*<br>**Bucket 2:** Parents meet on benches.<br>*Transition: "Ten years ago in 2016..."*<br>**Bucket 3:** 10th anniversary history. | **One Causal Chain:**<br>1. A toddler walks in and tackles a physical challenge (builds confidence/literacy)...<br>2. **BECAUSE** ten feet away on the bench, their exhausted parent feels safe and supported by other caregivers (a calm parent makes a brave kid)...<br>3. **THEREFORE**, what started at Fish Creek 10 years ago and grew to 13 locations isn't an indoor playground—it's a support system around the whole family. |
| **Spoken Voice** | *"When a toddler balances across this beam, they are crossing their physical midline, creating the neurological bridge to scan left to right."* (Textbook prose). | *"Watch a two-year-old spend five minutes trying to get a wooden gear to turn. They get frustrated, they try again, and then—it clicks. Long before a kid reads a word on a page, that's how they learn they can figure out a hard world."* (Anecdote $\rightarrow$ Meaning). |
| **A/V Relationship** | **Video:** Kids singing and playing.<br>**Audio:** *"We call them the five simple practices: Talk, Sing, Read, Write, and Play."* (Brochure recitation). | **Video:** 5-shot sequence of a toddler's stubborn concentration on a puzzle, cutting to their look of triumph back at their dad.<br>**Audio:** Stops for a 2-second `[NAT SOUND]` pocket as the wooden piece *clacks* into place. |
